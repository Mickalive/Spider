            <p>JSONPlaceholder is brilliant for getting started. But at some point, your app stops being a demo-and your fake API starts getting in the way.</p>
<p>This post is about recognizing that moment, and what to do next.</p>
<h2>What JSONPlaceholder does really well</h2>
<p>JSONPlaceholder shines when you need:</p>
<ul>
<li>Instant fake endpoints</li>
<li>Predictable JSON responses</li>
<li>Zero setup</li>
<li>Something to unblock UI work</li>
</ul>
<p>For tutorials, quick demos, and early prototyping, it&#39;s hard to beat.</p>
<h2>The moment it starts to fall apart</h2>
<p>Things usually break down when you need one of these:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Persistence</strong> (refresh the page and your data disappears)</li>
<li><strong>Per-user data</strong> (everyone sees the same fake records)</li>
<li><strong>Auth flows</strong> (login screens that don&#39;t lie)</li>
<li><strong>Logs</strong> (what actually happened?)</li>
<li><strong>Confidence</strong> that your frontend matches reality</li>
</ul>
<p>At this point, developers often reach for a full backend-or start wiring together half a dozen services.</p>
<h2>A middle ground: real endpoints without backend overhead</h2>
<p>This is where tools like ReqRes come in.</p>
<p>Instead of replacing JSONPlaceholder with a full backend, you get:</p>
<ul>
<li>Persistent JSON collections</li>
